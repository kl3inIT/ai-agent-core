Service Overview

The Epsilon service is a request-driven batch processor that consumes work items
from a durable queue and writes results to object storage. It runs on a horizontally
scaled worker pool behind a health-checked load balancer. Operational ownership
belongs to the platform reliability team, with secondary ownership from the data
engineering team for format and schema concerns.

Routine Health Checks

Operators inspect the service dashboard at the start of every shift. Key signals
include queue depth, worker saturation, error rate over the last fifteen minutes,
and output freshness. Any signal above its yellow threshold for more than five
minutes is escalated to the on-call engineer for investigation.

Incident Response

When the queue depth exceeds the critical threshold or the error rate exceeds two
percent, the on-call engineer begins the standard triage runbook. Step one is to
confirm that upstream producers have not accidentally flooded the queue. Step two
is to check for a recent deploy. Step three is to scale the worker pool while root
cause analysis continues in a dedicated incident channel.

Post-Incident Review

Every Sev1 or Sev2 incident generates a timeline document and a review meeting
within five working days. Findings are tracked in the shared action tracker with
named owners and target dates. The review is blameless and focused on systemic
improvement rather than individual conduct.
